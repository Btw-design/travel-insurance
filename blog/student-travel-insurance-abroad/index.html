Student Travel Insurance for Studying Abroad — Complete Guide for Indian Students

Why Universities Require Health Insurance

Studying abroad is a transformative experience for Indian students, but it comes with significant financial and health-related risks. Most universities across the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and Europe mandate that international students carry adequate health insurance. This requirement exists because domestic healthcare systems often do not cover international students, and medical costs abroad can be exceptionally high. A single hospital visit in the US can cost upwards of USD 10,000, while a basic student health insurance plan covers such expenses comprehensively.

Beyond institutional requirements, student travel insurance provides peace of mind for both students and their families. It covers medical emergencies, trip interruptions, lost baggage, and even repatriation in the worst-case scenario. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) regulates travel insurance products sold in India, ensuring transparent policy wordings, standardised terms, and a free-look period of 15 days for cancellations.

Common Coverage Requirements for International Students

Student travel insurance policies typically cover the following key areas:

Medical Emergency and Hospitalisation

This is the core component of any student plan. It covers hospitalisation, doctor consultations, surgery, prescription medicines, diagnostic tests, and emergency dental treatment. Most universities require a minimum medical cover of USD 100,000 for US-bound students, EUR 30,000 for Schengen countries, and equivalent amounts for other destinations. Medical evacuation to a suitable hospital and repatriation of remains to India are also standard inclusions.

Emergency Evacuation and Repatriation

If a student suffers a serious illness or injury that cannot be treated locally, the policy covers transportation to the nearest adequate medical facility or back to India. Repatriation of remains coverage ensures that in the tragic event of a student's death, the body is returned to India. These benefits are crucial for students studying in remote areas or countries with limited healthcare infrastructure.

Trip Cancellation and Interruption

If a student has to cancel or cut short their study abroad trip due to unforeseen events such as illness, visa refusal, death of a family member, or natural disasters, this cover reimburses non-refundable expenses including tuition fees, flight tickets, and accommodation deposits.

Baggage and Personal Belongings

Loss, theft, or damage to checked luggage, laptops, textbooks, and other personal belongings is covered up to specified limits. Many policies also include delayed baggage cover, providing funds to purchase essential items if baggage arrives more than 6 to 12 hours late.

Personal Accident and Liability

Personal accident cover provides a lump sum payment in case of accidental death or permanent total disability. Personal liability cover protects the student if they accidentally cause injury to another person or damage someone's property.

Policy Duration: Single-Trip vs Multi-Year Plans

Student travel insurance is available in two main variants. Single-trip policies cover one journey of up to 180 days, suitable for semester exchanges or short-term study programmes. Multi-year policies cover the entire duration of a degree programme, typically 1 to 5 years, and are ideal for full-time undergraduate or postgraduate students pursuing degrees abroad. Multi-year plans are generally more cost-effective than buying annual policies each year and ensure continuous coverage without gaps. Some insurers also offer extendable policies that allow students to renew for additional years if their course duration increases.

Pre-Existing Conditions for Students

Coverage for pre-existing medical conditions is a common concern for students. Most standard student travel insurance policies exclude pre-existing conditions. However, some insurers provide cover for pre-existing conditions if the condition is stable, fully disclosed at the time of purchase, and the insurer accepts the risk — often with an additional premium. It is critical to declare all pre-existing conditions honestly. Non-disclosure can lead to claim rejection later, which can be financially devastating when studying abroad. Students with chronic conditions such as asthma, diabetes, or thyroid disorders should specifically look for policies that offer pre-existing condition cover.

Destination-Specific Requirements for Indian Students

United States (F1 Visa)

US universities mandate health insurance for all international students on F1 visas. Most institutions offer their own student health insurance plans that automatically enrol international students. These plans typically provide coverage of at least USD 100,000 per accident or illness, include mental health benefits, prescription drug coverage, and emergency evacuation. Some universities allow students to waive the institutional plan if they have equivalent coverage from an Indian insurer, but the waiver is subject to strict criteria. The annual cost of US university-sponsored plans ranges from USD 1,500 to USD 3,000. Indian students should check with their international student office for waiver-eligible criteria before purchasing a separate policy. For additional information on studying in the US, visit EducationUSA.

United Kingdom (Tier 4 / Student Visa)

Students on a UK Tier 4 visa are required to pay the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) as part of their visa application, which grants access to the National Health Service (NHS). However, the NHS does not cover private treatment, dental care, optical services, or repatriation. Therefore, many Indian students opt for supplemental student travel insurance to cover these gaps. Typical plans cost between Rs. 8,000 and Rs. 15,000 per year. For official student advice, refer to UKCISA.

Canada

Canadian provinces have varying requirements. British Columbia, Alberta, and Saskatchewan include international students in their provincial health plans after a waiting period of 3 months. Other provinces like Ontario and Quebec do not cover international students, making private health insurance mandatory. Canadian universities often offer mandatory health insurance plans for international students. Minimum coverage typically ranges from CAD 50,000 to CAD 100,000.

Australia

Australia mandates Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the entire duration of the student visa. OSHC covers medical treatment, hospital visits, ambulance services, and limited pharmaceuticals. Indian students must maintain OSHC for the full period of their stay in Australia. OSHC costs approximately AUD 500 to AUD 700 per year for single cover. Some students also purchase additional travel insurance for extra benefits like baggage cover and trip cancellation.

Europe (Schengen Student Visa)

Students applying for a Schengen student visa must provide proof of travel insurance valid throughout the Schengen area with minimum medical coverage of EUR 30,000. This insurance must cover medical emergencies, emergency evacuation, and repatriation. For a detailed overview of Schengen requirements, see our guide on Schengen student visa insurance requirements.

Student Travel Insurance Checklist

Cost Expectations for Student Travel Insurance

The premium for student travel insurance depends on several factors: destination country (USA is the most expensive due to high medical costs, followed by Europe and Australia), sum insured selected, age of the student, trip duration, and optional add-ons. Below are typical annual premium ranges for Indian students:

  • United States: Rs. 15,000 to Rs. 40,000 per year
  • United Kingdom: Rs. 8,000 to Rs. 15,000 per year (supplemental to IHS)
  • Canada: Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 20,000 per year
  • Australia (OSHC): Rs. 12,000 to Rs. 25,000 per year
  • Europe (Schengen): Rs. 6,000 to Rs. 15,000 per year

Multi-year policies typically offer a 10-20% discount compared to buying separate annual plans. Students should also consider deductibles, co-payment clauses, and sub-limits when comparing prices.

Tips for Choosing the Right Student Plan

Selecting the right student travel insurance policy requires careful evaluation. Start by checking your university's specific requirements — some institutions mandate certain minimum coverage amounts or specific benefits. If your university offers a waiver option, compare the institutional plan with independent Indian insurer plans. Look for policies that include medical evacuation, repatriation, and liability cover as standard features. Verify the insurer's claim settlement ratio and network of cashless hospitals in your destination country. Purchase the policy immediately after receiving your visa or university acceptance to avail trip cancellation benefits. Always read the fine print — understand the exclusions, deductibles, and waiting periods. For ongoing medical needs, understand how senior citizen travel insurance differs in coverage approach (applicable for older students or parents accompanying them). Additionally, learn how to file a travel insurance claim before you travel so you are prepared in case of an emergency.

Conclusion

Student travel insurance is not just a visa or university requirement — it is an essential safety net for Indian students pursuing education abroad. From medical emergencies and trip cancellations to lost baggage and personal liability, a comprehensive policy provides financial protection and peace of mind throughout the academic journey. Understanding university-specific requirements, choosing the right coverage limits, declaring pre-existing conditions, and selecting a suitable policy duration are critical steps in making an informed decision. Always buy from an IRDAI-registered insurer, read the policy wording carefully, and keep emergency contact information handy during your stay abroad.
