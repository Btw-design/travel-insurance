How to File a Travel Insurance Claim: Step-by-Step Guide

Introduction

Filing a travel insurance claim can seem daunting, especially when you are dealing with the stress of a medical emergency, lost baggage, or a cancelled trip. Understanding the proper procedure and having the right documentation ready can make the difference between a smooth settlement and a rejected claim. This guide walks you through each step of the travel insurance claim process for Indian travellers.

Step 1: Notify Your Insurer Immediately

As soon as an incident occurs that may lead to a claim, notify your insurance company immediately. Most insurers have a 24/7 emergency assistance helpline that you can call from anywhere in the world. For medical emergencies, the insurer can coordinate cashless hospitalisation if the hospital is in their network. For non-medical claims like lost baggage or flight delays, the helpline can guide you on what steps to take. Delay in notification can be grounds for claim rejection, so do not wait.

Step 2: Collect All Required Documents

Proper documentation is the most critical part of the claim process. The documents you need will depend on the type of claim:

For Medical Claims

You will need original medical bills and receipts itemising all charges, discharge summary from the hospital, doctor's prescription and all medical reports including investigation and diagnostic reports, a copy of the insurance policy, a duly filled and signed claim form, copies of your passport pages showing personal details and immigration stamps, boarding passes and flight itinerary, and a police FIR if the injury or illness resulted from an accident, theft, or assault.

For Baggage Claims

You need a Property Irregularity Report (PIR) filed with the airline at the airport, boarding passes and baggage tags, a list of lost items with approximate values and purchase receipts if available, and the claim form. For delayed baggage, you also need a letter from the airline confirming the delay duration.

For Trip Cancellation Claims

You need the cancellation invoice from the airline, hotel, or tour operator showing the amount forfeited, proof of the reason for cancellation such as a medical certificate or death certificate, booking confirmations and receipts, and the completed claim form.

Step 3: Submit the Claim

Once you have all the documents, submit the claim online through the insurer's portal or by email. Most Indian insurers now accept digital submissions. Ensure all documents are clear and legible. Keep copies of everything you submit. Note the claim reference number provided by the insurer for future tracking. If submitting by physical mail, use registered post with tracking.

Step 4: Track the Claim Status

After submission, you can track your claim status using the reference number on the insurer's website or app. If you do not receive updates, follow up with the customer service team. As per IRDAI guidelines, the insurer must acknowledge receipt of your claim within 7 days and update you on its progress.

Step 5: Claim Settlement

IRDAI mandates that insurers must settle or reject a claim within 30 days of receiving all required documents. If approved, the claim amount is paid within 7 days of settlement approval. If the claim is rejected, the insurer must provide a detailed written reason for rejection. If additional investigation is required, the timeline can extend to 45 days with proper notice to you.

Tips for a Smooth Claim Process

Read your policy document before travelling so you understand what is covered and what is excluded. Keep a copy of the policy and emergency helpline number in your phone and printed in your bag. Notify the insurer before seeking treatment unless it is an absolute emergency. Keep all original documents and receipts. Take photographs of damaged baggage or accident scenes. Respond promptly to any queries from the insurance company. Maintain a claim diary noting dates and times of all communications with the insurer.

Common Mistakes That Lead to Claim Rejection

Failing to notify the insurer within the specified timeline is one of the most common reasons for claim rejection. Submitting incomplete or illegible documents is another frequent issue. Many claims are rejected because the treatment received was for a condition excluded under the policy such as a pre-existing disease not disclosed at purchase. Not obtaining prior approval for non-emergency treatment, claiming for expenses not covered, and submitting claims after the policy expiry date are other common pitfalls. Understanding these mistakes can help you avoid them.

What to Do If Your Claim Is Rejected

If your claim is rejected, first request a detailed written explanation from the insurer. If you believe the rejection is unfair, file a grievance with the insurer's internal grievance redressal officer. If the response is unsatisfactory, approach the Insurance Ombudsman in your jurisdiction. You can also file a complaint on the IRDAI IGMS portal or the Bima Bharosa website. As a last resort, you can approach the consumer court for redressal.

Conclusion

Filing a travel insurance claim requires timely action, thorough documentation, and patience. By following the steps outlined in this guide and understanding the applicable IRDAI regulations, you can navigate the claim process with confidence and maximise your chances of a successful settlement.
