Adventure Sports Travel Insurance — Cover for Trekking, Scuba Diving, Skiing & More

Introduction

Adventure sports tourism is growing rapidly among Indian travellers. From trekking in the Himalayas and scuba diving in the Andamans to skiing in Gulmarg and paragliding in Bir Billing, more Indians are seeking adrenaline-pumping experiences during their domestic and international travels. However, what many travellers do not realise is that most standard travel insurance policies exclude adventure sports from their coverage. A simple fall while trekking at altitude or a decompression sickness incident while diving can result in massive out-of-pocket medical expenses. This guide explains everything Indian travellers need to know about adventure sports travel insurance, including which activities are covered, which are excluded, what add-ons are available, and how to choose the right coverage for your adventure trip.

Which Adventure Sports Are Typically Excluded?

Standard travel insurance policies have a list of excluded activities. While exclusions vary by insurer, the following adventure sports are commonly excluded from regular coverage:

  • Trekking and mountaineering above 4,000–5,000 metres altitude
  • Scuba diving beyond 30 metres depth or without certification
  • Bungee jumping and bridge swinging
  • Skydiving and parachuting
  • Paragliding and hang gliding
  • Skiing and snowboarding (off-piste/backcountry)
  • White-water rafting above Grade III rapids
  • Rock climbing and ice climbing
  • Heli-skiing and heli-boarding
  • BASE jumping
  • Motorised adventure sports such as jet-skiing, ATV riding, and dirt biking
  • Zorbing and zip-lining (varies by insurer)

This is not an exhaustive list. Always read your policy's exclusion clause carefully. For a broader understanding of what standard policies typically exclude, see our guide on common exclusions in travel insurance.

Which Activities Are Covered Under Standard Policies?

Not all physically active pursuits are considered "adventure sports" by insurers. The following activities are usually covered under a standard travel insurance policy without needing an add-on:

  • Hiking on marked trails below 4,000 metres
  • Snorkeling (surface swimming with mask and fins, not diving)
  • Banana boat rides and similar inflatable water activities
  • Nature walks and wildlife safaris (in vehicles)
  • Recreational cycling on paved roads
  • Kayaking and canoeing on calm waters
  • Yoga retreats and wellness tourism
  • Scenic cable car rides

Even for covered activities, altitude and depth limits apply. A policy may cover hiking up to 4,000 metres but exclude anything beyond that. Read the fine print.

Adventure Sports Add-On Covers

Most Indian insurers offer an adventure sports add-on that can be purchased along with a standard travel insurance policy. Dedicated adventure travel insurance policies are also available from select insurers. These add-ons typically cover:

  • Medical expenses arising from adventure sports injuries
  • Emergency evacuation from remote or high-altitude locations
  • Search and rescue costs up to a specified limit
  • Repatriation of remains in case of accidental death
  • Personal accident cover with higher sums insured for adventure activities

Adventure sports add-ons cost between Rs. 500 and Rs. 2,500 depending on the number and type of activities covered, the destination, and the duration of the trip. Some insurers allow you to select specific activities to cover, while others offer a bundled adventure package.

Emergency Evacuation Coverage

One of the most important features of adventure sports travel insurance is emergency evacuation cover. If you are trekking in a remote Himalayan region and suffer an injury, or if a scuba diving accident requires decompression treatment, the cost of evacuating you to a proper medical facility can run into lakhs of rupees. Adventure sports policies typically include evacuation by helicopter, ambulance, or specialised medical transport. Some policies also cover the cost of a companion's travel and accommodation if you are hospitalised abroad for an extended period. Divers should also be aware of organisations like Divers Alert Network (DAN), which provides specialised diving safety and insurance resources.

How to Read Policy Wording for Adventure Exclusions

Travel insurance for Indian travellers is regulated by IRDAI, which requires all policies to have clear and transparent wordings. To understand whether your adventure activity is covered, follow these steps:

  1. Locate the "Exclusions" section — This is usually a clearly marked section in the policy document. Look for headings like "What is Not Covered" or "General Exclusions."
  2. Check for "Adventure Sports" or "Hazardous Activities" clauses — Insurers list excluded activities by name. If your activity appears in this list, it is not covered under the standard policy.
  3. Review altitude and depth limits — Many policies cover trekking up to a specific altitude (e.g., 4,000 metres) and scuba diving up to a specific depth (e.g., 30 metres). Activities beyond these limits are excluded.
  4. Look for certification requirements — Some policies require that scuba diving or paragliding be conducted with a licensed operator or that you hold a valid certification. Unsupervised or uncertified activities are excluded.
  5. Review the "Add-Ons" or "Rider" section — If you need coverage for excluded activities, check whether the insurer offers an adventure sports add-on and what specific activities it covers.

If you have a pre-existing medical conditions coverage situation, note that standard adventure add-ons typically do not cover complications arising from pre-existing conditions during adventure activities. You may need specialised coverage.

Tips for Adventure Travellers

  • Buy insurance at the time of booking — Just like regular travel insurance, adventure policies offer trip cancellation benefits only if purchased immediately after booking. Do not wait until the last minute.
  • Disclose all planned activities — When buying the policy, inform the insurer about all adventure activities you plan to undertake. Non-disclosure can result in claim rejection.
  • Carry proof of certification — For scuba diving, paragliding, or other certified activities, carry your certification card and proof that the activity was conducted with a licensed operator.
  • Understand the sub-limits — Even with an add-on, evacuation and medical sub-limits may be lower for adventure-related claims compared to regular medical claims.
  • Know the emergency numbers — Save the insurer's 24/7 emergency assistance number in your phone and share your itinerary with a family member.
  • Check for geographical limits — Some policies only cover adventure activities in specific regions or countries. Verify that your destination is included.
  • Follow the Adventure Travel Association safety guidelines for responsible adventure tourism.

Conclusion

Adventure sports add excitement to travel but also come with inherent risks. Standard travel insurance policies exclude most high-risk activities, making it essential for adventure-seeking travellers to purchase appropriate coverage. By understanding which activities are excluded, what add-ons are available, and how to read policy wordings, you can ensure you are adequately protected without paying for coverage you do not need. Always buy from an IRDAI-registered insurer, read the policy document thoroughly, and carry proof of coverage during your trip. For more guidance on making claims, refer to our claim process guide.
